London has four big airports, and for many trips only one of them flies your route nonstop. Searching "London" on a booking site mixes them together and hides which one that is.

This guide is built from flights we actually saw depart **Heathrow (LHR)**, **Gatwick (LGW)**, **Stansted (STN)** and **Luton (LTN)** between 16 September and 16 October 2026, using flight records from AeroDataBox. It is a 30-day snapshot of what really flew, not a list of announced routes. You can explore each network on a map: [Heathrow](/from/LHR), [Gatwick](/from/LGW), [Stansted](/from/STN) and [Luton](/from/LTN).

**London City (LCY) and Southend (SEN) are not covered.** They are not in our set of airports with observed flight data, so we cannot say what they flew in this window, and we will not guess from older schedules.

Distances are great-circle figures between airport coordinates (our calculation). "Sun destinations" means Spain, Portugal, Greece, Turkey, Cyprus, Malta, Egypt, Morocco, Tunisia, Cape Verde, Croatia, Montenegro, Albania and Bulgaria. Flight totals count every departure on a qualifying route, so a few private jets sharing a busy route are included.

Together the four airports reached 363 different destination airports. Only 41 of them were served from all four, and 193 were served from just one. That second number is the reason this guide exists.

British Airways was the only airline we saw on 77 Heathrow routes. Ryanair (with Ryanair UK) was alone on 106 Stansted routes, and Wizz Air on 36 Luton routes.

For Nordic travellers, the split is clear. Copenhagen, Oslo and Stockholm were flown from Heathrow (British Airways), Gatwick (Norwegian, plus easyJet to Copenhagen) and Stansted (Ryanair), but not from Luton. Helsinki had 137 flights from Heathrow on Finnair, against 34 from Stansted and 21 from Gatwick. Bergen, Stavanger and Trondheim were Gatwick-only.

## Getting there from central London

As the crow flies from Charing Cross, Heathrow is about 14.6 mi (23.5 km) away, Gatwick 25.0 mi (40.2 km), Luton 27.4 mi (44.1 km) and Stansted 30.4 mi (48.9 km) (our calculation). Rail links matter more than distance:

| Airport | Fastest rail link | Journey time and frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Heathrow | Heathrow Express from Paddington | About 15 minutes, every 15 minutes ([Heathrow](https://www.heathrow.com/transport-and-directions/getting-to-central-london)) |
| Heathrow | Elizabeth line | Under 45 minutes to central London, every 30 minutes ([Heathrow](https://www.heathrow.com/transport-and-directions/getting-to-central-london)) |
| Heathrow | London Underground (Piccadilly line) | Under an hour, every 10 minutes ([Heathrow](https://www.heathrow.com/transport-and-directions/getting-to-central-london)) |
| Gatwick | Gatwick Express from Victoria | 30 minutes nonstop, twice an hour ([Gatwick](https://gatwickairport.com/rail.html)) |
| Gatwick | Southern and Thameslink | 30 to 45 minutes; Thameslink serves London Bridge, Blackfriars, City Thameslink, Farringdon and St Pancras ([Gatwick](https://gatwickairport.com/rail.html)) |
| Stansted | Stansted Express from Liverpool Street | Average 48 minutes, most off-peak trains 47; every 15 minutes ([RailAdvent, citing Greater Anglia](https://railadvent.co.uk/2023/12/stansted-express-trains-to-run-every-fifteen-minutes-all-day.html)) |
| Luton | Train from St Pancras to Luton Airport Parkway, then the DART shuttle | 32 minutes to the terminal on the fastest non-stop train, about 40 on Thameslink; the DART itself is a three-minute ride ([Head for Points](https://www.headforpoints.com/2023/02/25/luton-airports-dart-train-launches-10th-march)) |

Two practical points. Heathrow Express arrives at Paddington, not the West End, so add a connection. And at Luton, buy a ticket to "Luton Airport" rather than "Luton Airport Parkway": the airport ticket includes the DART fare, which otherwise costs extra ([Head for Points](https://www.headforpoints.com/2023/02/25/luton-airports-dart-train-launches-10th-march)). Timetables change and engineering works close lines on weekends, so check before an early flight.

## Who should not bother crossing London

Pick the airport on your side of town when the route is everywhere:

- **Spain, Portugal and the big Mediterranean islands.** Malaga, Barcelona, Faro, Palma, Ibiza and Tenerife South were flown from all four, and Alicante from all but Heathrow. Choose by the airport nearest you, then by airline.
- **Dublin, Edinburgh and Glasgow.** All four airports run them daily. Belfast is split: Belfast City from Heathrow only, Belfast International from the other three.
- **Major European capitals.** Amsterdam, Paris Charles de Gaulle, Madrid, Lisbon, Prague, Budapest and Athens were served from all four, though Stansted had only nine flights to Paris in the window.
- **Istanbul and Tel Aviv.** Both were served from all four airports.

Cross town for the routes in the "only from" lists: Heathrow for almost anything in North America, Asia or Africa, Gatwick for the Caribbean and western Norway, Stansted for Ryanair's regional Europe, Luton for Romania.

## How to read the counts

A 30-day count converts to a weekly frequency if you divide by about 4.3. So 9 flights is roughly twice a week, 30 is about daily, and 100 or more is three or more a day.

Low counts in a mid-September to mid-October window usually mean a summer route winding down. Gatwick had 27 destinations with 13 or fewer flights, Stansted 33, Heathrow and Luton 16 each. Many are Greek islands and smaller French and Italian airports, like Stansted to Samos or Gatwick to Bastia (9 each). Expect some to pause over winter, and check the airline's timetable for your dates.

## About this data

Flights were recorded by AeroDataBox and aggregated by us for departures from LHR, LGW, STN and LTN between 16 September and 16 October 2026. "Flights observed" counts flights, not seats or passengers. We left out routes flown only by business jets, cargo airlines, government aircraft or ad-hoc charters, routes between London airports, and UK domestic legs flown only by holiday airlines. Airline names are as reported, so a route can list a parent airline and its subsidiary separately. Distances are great-circle calculations between coordinates. London City and Southend are not in our observed set.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which London airport has flights to the United States?

Heathrow, by a wide margin: 31 US destinations in our window. Gatwick had New York JFK (Norse Atlantic), Orlando and Tampa. Stansted and Luton had none.

### Is Gatwick or Heathrow better for a beach holiday?

Gatwick. 54% of its flights went to sun destinations, against 14% at Heathrow, and it had the Caribbean leisure routes Heathrow lacked, like Cancun, Punta Cana and Antigua.

### Can I fly long haul from Stansted or Luton?

Barely. Stansted's furthest route was Dubai on Emirates, and Luton's was Hurghada in Egypt on easyJet. Neither had flights to the Americas, South Asia or East Asia.

### Which London airport is quickest to reach from the city?

Heathrow, if you start near Paddington: the Heathrow Express takes about 15 minutes. Luton is 32 minutes from St Pancras on the fastest train, Gatwick 30 minutes from Victoria, and Stansted about 47 to 48 minutes from Liverpool Street.

### Why are London City and Southend missing?

They are not among the airports we have current observed flight data for, so we left them out rather than guess. Search them separately, and use our guide on [how to find direct flights](/blog/how-to-find-direct-flights) to check every London airport for your dates.
