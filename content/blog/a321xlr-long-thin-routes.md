For most of the jet age, crossing an ocean nonstop meant a widebody: a twin-aisle aircraft with 250 or more seats. That worked between big cities. It did not work between, say, Dublin and Indianapolis, because no airline could fill a 787 on that route every day of the year.

The Airbus A321XLR changes that maths. It is a single-aisle aircraft, the same family as the A320 you take on short European hops, with enough range to fly many transatlantic routes. Airlines call the routes it opens "long and thin": long enough to need serious range, thin enough in demand that a smaller aircraft is the only way to make them pay.

This guide explains what the XLR is, which airlines fly it in 2026, and what that means for the nonstop options from your airport.

## What the A321XLR is

The A321XLR ("Xtra Long Range") is the longest-range version of the A321neo. Its key addition is a permanent rear centre fuel tank built into the fuselage, which adds about 12,900 litres of fuel capacity and sits [behind the main undercarriage bay](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/stories/2020-07-first-metal-cut-for-the-first-a321xlrs-rear-centre-tank-at-airbus), taking up less cargo-hold space than the removable extra tanks used on earlier long-range A321s. Airbus quotes a range of [up to 4,700 nautical miles](https://airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-10-airbus-delivers-first-a321xlr-to-iberia), about 8,700 km and 15% more than the earlier A321LR.

That headline figure assumes particular payload and conditions. On a real flight, a strong headwind or a full load of passengers and bags reduces what the aircraft can do, so airlines plan XLR routes with margin. In practice, most XLR routes so far are between about 4,500 and 6,500 km.

Certification took longer than planned. Regulators wanted assurances about the safety of a large fuel tank under the cabin floor, and the [EASA type certificate](https://aviationweek.com/air-transport/safety-ops-regulation/easa-certifies-leap-powered-airbus-a321xlr) for the CFM-powered version was issued on 19 July 2024, with deliveries starting a few months later.

## Single aisle versus widebody

Here is how the XLR compares with the types it competes with or replaces. Seat counts vary by airline; the XLR figures are real airline layouts.

| | A321XLR | Boeing 757-200 (the old narrowbody option) | Typical long-haul widebody |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aisles | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| Seats | 155 (American, 3 cabins) to 182 (Iberia, 2 cabins) | about 200 in two classes | usually well over 200 |
| Still in production | Yes | No | Yes |
| Lie-flat business seats | Offered by several airlines | Some airlines | Standard on most long-haul |
| Best fit | Long, thin routes | Long, thin routes (aging fleet) | Busy long-haul routes |

The 757 is worth a mention. Airlines such as United have flown transatlantic routes with it for years, but [production ended in 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_757) and the fleet is old. United's XLRs [replace its 757-200s](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/170976), with 32 premium seats per aircraft, 16 more than the 757s they replace.

## Why a smaller aircraft opens new routes

An airline launching a nonstop route has to fill enough seats every day, at a decent average fare, to cover the cost of the aircraft and crew. A widebody has more seats to fill. On a route with modest demand, the airline either fills them with cheap fares, flies a few days a week, or does not fly at all. That is why so many mid-sized cities have long needed a connection through a hub to cross an ocean.

The XLR cuts the number of seats per flight roughly in half compared with a widebody, and it costs less to operate per trip. That has three effects:

1. **New city pairs.** Routes like Dublin to Nashville or Newark to Luxembourg become possible because 150 to 180 seats a day is a realistic target.
2. **Year-round or more frequent service.** A route that could support a widebody only in summer may support a smaller aircraft for more of the year, or twice daily instead of once.
3. **Hubs reaching further.** Airlines can connect a large hub to a secondary city on another continent, feeding connections at the hub end.

## Who flies the XLR and where (as of October 2026)

Dates below are as announced by the airlines or reported at the time. Schedules change, so check before you book.

### Iberia

Iberia was the launch operator. Airbus [delivered its first A321XLR on 30 October 2024](https://airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-10-airbus-delivers-first-a321xlr-to-iberia), configured with 182 seats in two classes, including lie-flat business seats with direct aisle access. After a short spell on European routes, Madrid to Boston was planned as its first transatlantic route in November 2024. That route is about 5,470 km by great circle (our calculation). See [Madrid](/from/MAD) and [Boston](/from/BOS) on the map.

### Aer Lingus

Aer Lingus, part of the same group as Iberia, took its first two XLRs in December 2024 and planned [new routes from Dublin to Nashville and Indianapolis](https://airlinegeeks.com/2024/12/18/aer-lingus-receives-its-first-a321xlr/) for spring 2025, each four times a week at launch. Both are classic long, thin routes: mid-sized US cities that had no nonstop link to Ireland. For summer 2026 the airline [increased both to five flights a week](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/aer-lingus-expands-transatlantic-network-with-new-destination) and added Raleigh-Durham as a year-round XLR route from 13 April 2026. Explore [Dublin](/from/DUB) to see how its network looked in the archive.

### American Airlines

American was the first US airline to operate the type. Its [first XLR flight, on 18 December 2025](https://news.aa.com/news/news-details/2025/A-suite-first-flight-Americans-new-Airbus-A321XLR-debuts-on-transcontinental-route-FLT-12/default.aspx), was a transcontinental New York JFK to Los Angeles service, with 20 Flagship Suite seats, 12 premium economy and 123 main cabin seats. Its first transatlantic XLR route, [New York JFK to Edinburgh](https://aeroxplorer.com/articles/american-airlines-single-aisle-gamble-finally-crosses-the-atlantic.php), began on 8 March 2026 as a seasonal service scheduled through 24 October 2026. It is about 5,230 km. See [JFK](/from/JFK) and [Edinburgh](/from/EDI).

### United Airlines

United took its first XLR in 2026 and planned to use it on domestic routes from September 2026, moving to international service on 1 December 2026 with [Washington Dulles to Amsterdam and Dublin](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/170976). New XLR routes announced for 2027 are Newark to Luxembourg (from 2 April), Washington Dulles to Toulouse (from 26 April), Newark to Ibiza and Valencia (from late May and early June) and Newark to Marseille (from 4 June). Dulles to Toulouse is about 6,350 km, at the longer end of what the type is being used for. See [Dulles](/from/IAD) and [Newark](/from/EWR).

### Air Canada

Air Canada's XLRs arrived late: the first was delayed from late 2025 into 2026. It launched XLR service from Montreal with [seasonal flights to Palma de Mallorca, four a week from 17 June 2026](https://atxjetsetter.com/post/air-canada-reveals-initial-airbus-a321xlr-routes/), but [only eight June round trips were planned on the XLR](https://aeroxplorer.com/articles/air-canada-is-about-to-fly-directly-from-montreal-to-mallorca-for-the-first-time), with a Boeing 787-8 flying most of the summer. The airline also plans to move its Montreal routes to Toulouse (becoming year-round) and Edinburgh onto the type. Its schedule also [shows the XLR on Halifax to London Heathrow](https://airlinegeeks.com/2025/12/01/air-canada-adds-new-a321xlr-route/) between 30 September and 24 October 2026, temporarily replacing a Boeing 737 MAX. The Palma schedule shows how a new route can start on whatever aircraft is available, but the long-term logic is the XLR's: a summer widebody to Palma is one thing, while a year-round Toulouse service from Montreal is the kind of route where a smaller aircraft fits demand outside the peak. See [Montreal](/from/YUL).

### IndiGo

India's largest airline received its first A321XLR in January 2026 and [scheduled it on Mumbai and Delhi to Athens](https://networkthoughts.com/2026/01/15/the-xlr-induction-solves-the-turkish-problem-for-indigo/), three times a week each from 23 January 2026. Istanbul and Bali were named as the next XLR destinations. IndiGo has 40 XLRs on order and expected nine in 2026, which makes it a carrier to watch for new nonstops between India and Europe.

### Qantas

Qantas uses the XLR differently. Its [first commercial XLR flights on 25 September 2025](https://www.airdatanews.com/qantas-begins-commercial-flights-with-airbus-a321xlr-in-australia/) were domestic, Sydney to Melbourne and Sydney to Perth, where the range lets one aircraft type cover the whole country, including the long transcontinental sectors. There the XLRs replace Boeing 737-800s, which have a range of about 5,700 km against the XLR's 8,700 km.

## What it means for travellers

**More nonstop options from secondary cities.** If you live near a mid-sized airport, the XLR is the aircraft most likely to bring you a new transatlantic or Asia to Europe nonstop in the next few years.

**A different cabin experience.** A single-aisle aircraft on a seven or eight hour flight feels different from a widebody. There is one aisle, fewer lavatories per passenger and a narrower cabin. In business class, several XLR operators offer lie-flat seats, and Iberia's have direct aisle access. In economy, the seat pitch and width are set by each airline, so look at the specific airline's layout before you book.

**Fewer seats, sometimes higher fares.** A smaller aircraft on a thin route means less capacity to discount. The nonstop may cost more than a connection through a hub. Our guide to [nonstop versus connecting flights](/blog/nonstop-vs-connecting-flights) helps you weigh the time saved against the price.

**Watch for seasonal routes.** Many XLR routes start as summer-only services. If a route disappears in winter, it may come back in spring.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far can the A321XLR fly?

Airbus quotes a range of up to 4,700 nautical miles, about 8,700 km. Real-world range depends on passenger and cargo load and on winds, so airlines typically plan XLR routes well inside that limit.

### Is the A321XLR comfortable on a long flight?

It depends on the airline's cabin. Several operators fit lie-flat business seats, and the A321 cabin is a little wider than a Boeing 737's. It is still a single-aisle aircraft, so the cabin is narrower and has fewer lavatories than a widebody.

### Which airline flew the A321XLR first?

Iberia. Airbus delivered the first aircraft on 30 October 2024, and Iberia planned its first transatlantic XLR flights, Madrid to Boston, for November 2024.

### Why do airlines like the XLR for new routes?

It carries roughly half the passengers of a typical widebody at lower cost per trip. That makes it possible to fly nonstop between cities that cannot fill a widebody every day, and to run more frequencies on routes that could only support a few large-aircraft flights a week.

### Will the XLR replace widebodies?

On busy long-haul routes, no. Widebodies carry more passengers and cargo and fly much further. The XLR fills a gap below them, mostly on routes that would otherwise need a connection.
